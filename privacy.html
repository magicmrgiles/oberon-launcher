Privacy policy

Effective October 30, 2026

Oberon Launcher is an Android home screen app made by magicmrgiles. This policy explains what it reads, what it stores, and where data goes.

The short version

  • Oberon Launcher has no server and no user accounts of its own, so the developer never receives your data.
  • It contains no ads, analytics or crash reporting.
  • It doesn't sell your data, and only sends it to the services listed below when you use the features that need them.

Gmail data

Connecting a Gmail account is optional. If you connect one, Oberon Launcher asks Google for the gmail.modify permission and uses it to:

  • read your email address, to label the account in the app;
  • read your unread inbox conversations: the sender, subject, time and the short preview Gmail provides, but not the full message body or attachments;
  • mark a conversation as read, or archive it, when you ask it to.

It does not send, delete or draft email.

Where Gmail data goes. Your phone talks directly to Google. Gmail data is not sent to the developer or to any other third party.

What is kept. Message details are held in memory to display them and are not written to storage. A few things are saved on your phone:

  • your connected email addresses, and which ones you've marked as work;
  • identifiers of conversations you've dismissed or asked to be reminded about, which don't include their content;
  • the text of any task you choose to create from an email, which starts as the sender and subject or preview.

The access permission itself is held by Google Play services, not by the app.

Limited Use. Oberon Launcher's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Gmail data is used only to provide the features described above. It is not used for advertising, not read by people, and not used to train AI models.

Other data on your phone

Each of these is only used if you turn on the related feature, and stays on your phone unless stated below.

  • Notifications: with your permission, it reads your notifications to show messages in the Communication hub and let you reply. They're held in memory while they're showing.
  • Calendar: it reads your events to show them.
  • Installed apps: it lists your apps so it can open them and let you choose which to hide.
  • Accessibility service: the optional "Hide while typing" setting only checks whether a keyboard is showing. It doesn't read what's on your screen or what you type.
  • Your own content: tasks, notes, habits and settings are stored on your phone.

Services the app connects to

Features you use send the minimum they need directly to these services, under their own privacy policies:

  • Google: Gmail and sign-in, as above, and downloading fonts you choose.
  • Open-Meteo: your approximate location or the city you typed, to get the weather.
  • Chess.com: the public username you enter, to find your games.
  • The New York Times: if you sign in to your NYT account inside the app, to show your puzzle progress.

Backups

If Android backup is on for your phone, Android may copy the app's settings and its tasks, notes and habits to your own Google account, or to a new phone when you transfer. That includes the saved items listed under Gmail data above. You control this in your phone's backup settings. The developer can't access these backups.

Removing your data

  • Sign out of a Gmail account in the app's email account settings, or remove Oberon Launcher's access at your Google Account permissions page.
  • Uninstall the app to delete everything it stored on your phone. Any backup in your Google account is managed through your Google and phone settings.

Children

Oberon Launcher isn't directed at children under 13.
